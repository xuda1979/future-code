\section{Productivity Boundary and Research Scope}
\label{sec:productivity}
This manuscript and its companion review were produced together. The governing
research objective is to reduce the cost and time required for agents to complete
large software projects correctly, without increasing coordination failures or
weakening acceptance criteria. It is therefore useful to state, before the
method, what the present evidence does---and does not---establish about that
objective.

\paragraph{The certificate problem is not yet a productivity result.}
Adaptive HACT is an evidence-serialization component: it optimizes the
\emph{layout} that serializes verification evidence under fixed test execution
schedules, separating immutable check identities from the mutable execution
state that records a run. It does not change the agent's planning,
repository exploration, reasoning history, number of failed patches, or
allocation of work; it does not schedule, reorder, or parallelize checks. A
strong result on the certificate objective can therefore
coexist with no improvement in whole-project productivity, and the manuscript
acknowledges exactly this boundary throughout. Historical checker timings were
essentially flat (39.69\,s versus 39.77\,s for fixed and learned layouts), and a
separate small harness paid roughly five seconds per episode for strict
verification versus direct pytest. These are \emph{negative controls}: they
show that a smaller certificate transcript is not automatically a faster build,
a better agent, or a smaller root-status prompt, and they delimit the
serialization claim rather than support any acceleration claim.

\paragraph{Where the measurement stops.}
No hosted model was called; no physical WAN and no actual public-vocabulary BPE
encoding were evaluated; and there is no new SWE-bench or natural historical-bug
cohort. The 288 loopback transfers belong to an earlier version and use
application pacing rather than real network conditions. Consequently this paper
does not claim a latency, token, or billing win. Its contribution is a scoped
systems component: when protected hierarchical evidence must cross a constrained
link, a learned-order balanced layout can reduce the bytes that must cross it,
and we provide the accounting, the codec-matched test, and the honesty about
what remains unmeasured.

\paragraph{The direction implied by the review.}
The review recommends treating this certificate result as the evidence
\emph{backend} of a larger program about verified-frontier context retirement,
dependency-aware parallelism, and task granularity. That program is a design and
evaluation protocol in progress, not a completed experiment; Section~11 of the
review and our discussion articulate the concrete experiment that would promote
this component to a productivity claim. We do not relabel the scripted evidence
below as an evaluation of that larger controller.
