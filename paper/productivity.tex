\section{Productivity Boundary and Research Scope}\label{sec:productivity}
This manuscript and its companion review were produced together. The
governing research objective is to reduce the cost and time required
for agents to complete large software projects correctly, without
increasing coordination failures or weakening acceptance
contracts. That objective outruns what any single paper can measure,
and this version is explicit about the ladder of evidence it occupies.

\paragraph{What changed in this version.}
Version 5.1 presented HACT as an evidence-serialization component and
labeled the retirement question a future protocol. The review's
recommendation was to make that protocol the paper or to publish HACT
as a scoped component. This version does the former.
Section~\ref{sec:retire} executes the pre-registered context-
retirement study: ten arms, 630 deterministic runs, three closure
rules frozen before data generation. The result is asymmetric and is
reported as measured. The null closure \emph{triggered}: frontier
retirement does not beat strong observation masking on submitted
bytes, and no byte-cost productivity claim is made. What survives is
(i)~a correctness falsification---periodic summary/reset, the cheapest
policy on bytes, fails the frozen acceptance contract on 42 of 63 runs
through silent staleness and lost obligations, while retention
policies hold 63/63; (ii)~a 48.3\% reduction in on-demand retrieval
operations at equal correctness, pre-registering the live-cohort
hypothesis that savings come from tool-call round trips, not bytes;
(iii)~a conditional decision rule for certificate-tree evidence
layouts, which win on dense updates and lose on sparse ones. HACT
leaves the paper's center: it survives only where its ablation says it
matters, and Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:eval} now carry it as
a scoped component with unchanged scope labels.

\paragraph{The boundary that remains.}
The retirement study is mechanism-level. It models information flow
between a context policy and a seeded work stream; it does not call a
hosted model, and it cannot establish that any agent finishes faster,
bills less, or reasons better. Its staleness and obligation-loss
results are structural predictions about policies, not measurements of
agents. The live-agent cohort that could test those predictions has
its protocol pre-registered in Section~\ref{sec:live-protocol} and has
not been run. The negative controls below---flat checker timings,
per-episode verification overhead, unchanged root-status inputs---are
reported as limits of the serialization claim, and the retirement
cohort's own closures are reported as limits of the mechanism claim.
Nothing in this paper is an agent-productivity measurement, and the
abstract says so.
