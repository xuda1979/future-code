\section{Deployment Implications, Limitations, and Next Experiments}\label{sec:limitations}
\paragraph{Keep reasoning distributed, make certification explicit.}
Workers should retain narrow task contexts, while deterministic software handles ownership, dependency closure, status counts, and publication eligibility. A reasoning coordinator can inspect bounded certificate cards and selectively retrieve implicated traces. It should not invent a global success claim from child prose. HACT supplies only the certificate component of this architecture. Existing context-packet controls must still constrain the rest of each worker's request.

\paragraph{Changing the tree is not free.}
Our metric is steady-state refresh traffic. Constructing a new layout requires planning and initializing its internal records; switching may also create scheduling or audit overhead. If a proposed tree is expected to save $d>0$ bytes per future episode and its migration costs $B_{\mathrm{switch}}$ bytes, the simplest break-even count is $B_{\mathrm{switch}}/d$. This arithmetic is not a validated online switching policy. A deployable policy should estimate savings on independent recent batches, account for drift and planning cost in compatible units, and require a margin before replacement. The prototype validates safe reuse when trees switch; it does not implement or evaluate autonomous switch timing.

\paragraph{A small verification context is not a small total intelligence requirement.}
A conjunction of trusted gate verdicts has an exact compact aggregate. Arbitrary design tradeoffs, theorem discovery, research assessment, and natural-language correctness generally do not. In particular, an encoder supporting exact recovery of every bit of an unrestricted $n$-bit state without retrieval must distinguish $2^n$ states and needs at least $n$ bits. Durable evidence and selective reads relocate information; they do not make it disappear. HACT therefore makes no claim that every difficult project can be solved by short independent conversations.

\paragraph{Contract coverage and checker trust.}
The kernel cannot repair an incomplete acceptance contract, hidden data dependency, compromised checker, or misleading test. Its hashes are not signatures, remote attestation, or proofs of computation. The fixture's mutations stay within syntactically valid, pure arithmetic modules. Untrusted code needs a genuine sandbox and controlled side effects; a JSON input, subprocess boundary, or local lock alone is not sufficient isolation.

\paragraph{Statistical and external validity.}
Synthetic groups are deliberately constructed to expose co-invalidation structure; they are not observations of industrial agent traces. The executable fixture is small and highly controlled. Its full oracle shares predicate definitions with the selective checker. Seed-level intervals describe variation under these generators and do not provide confidence intervals for industrial repair quality. Recent agent and authenticated-tree preprints cited here may change. The study does not establish state-of-the-art reasoning performance, production savings, or superiority over full implementations of DeLM or HMT.

\paragraph{Required real-agent evaluation.}
The next substantive test should freeze real repository tasks and gate registries, collect invalidation episodes from actual model-generated edits, and compare fixed, marginal, and HACT certificate layouts under the same model, prompts, tool permissions, seeds, and aggregate budget. Required endpoints include verified task success, false publication attempts, request tokens, repeated evidence retrieval, end-to-end latency, migration cost, and traffic. These measurements would determine whether certificate savings are material relative to inference and tool costs. They have not been executed in this manuscript and are not filled in with synthetic agent scores.

\section{Conclusion}
The relevant alternative to one large managerial context is not simply a larger hierarchy. It is a separation between distributed reasoning and explicit, bounded verification. HACT models repeated gate invalidation as correlated set activity and chooses an ordered certificate tree under a packet budget. Its core optimization is an attributable specialization of classical alphabetic dynamic programming; its information separation explains why marginal activity can be insufficient. The trusted kernel preserves declared coverage and freshness independently of the chosen tree, including after topology changes.

Executed experiments support a limited but concrete conclusion: for the tested correlated workloads, HACT reduces certificate-refresh traffic without changing the observed verification predicate. Independent and global controls, sparse-data depth problems, and a drift regression delimit that benefit. A live-model study remains necessary before extending these results to autonomous software-engineering quality or total agent-system efficiency.
