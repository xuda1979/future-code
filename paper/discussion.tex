\section{Deployment Recommendation, Limitations, and Next Experiments}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Deployment recommendation}\label{sec:deploy}
Choose a service before an algorithm. Do not deploy the strict wrapper solely on
the strength of these transmission savings. For local trusted status, use the
root projection; for final per-test states, a flat ledger may suffice. Where
protected hierarchical evidence is already required, enable a suitable codec,
retain the incumbent, and consider learned-order balanced fixed8 as the
low-fitting-cost candidate. Exact DP remains an offline reference and can be
worthwhile when its extra byte reduction pays back. Block DP is an ablation, not
the default.

A cost calculator is not measured payback. The new scenarios expose when assumed
extra costs dominate compressed delivery savings. They neither import a
five-second overhead from a different fixture as though paired with each source
case nor assume unlimited repeat exports. Dynamic adaptation is optional; the
priced window selects already compiled candidates and can regress under rapid
change. Whole-epoch compression changes streaming latency, and high-latency links
do not necessarily favor HACT. Those are deployment questions, not consequences of
the tree objective.

\paragraph{New default, new evidence boundary.}
The practical recommendation changed after the v4 review. It is therefore tested
on new frozen synthetic seeds with disjoint train/validation/held-out draws. The
twelve source candidates are reused diagnostic evidence and are not an independent
test of model-generated repairs. On these replays, learned balanced trees are
smaller than original-order fixed8 yet larger than exact learned layouts. The
recommendation is about compilation simplicity, not universal superiority. Three
seeds per setting support descriptive ranges, not precise generalization claims.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
Unclosed empirical requests: neither a physical WAN nor actual public-vocabulary
BPE was evaluated in this release. Software/vocabulary retrieval failed, and no
authorized pair of remote controlled hosts was available. The optional tools and
local tests do not close these experimental gaps. No hosted model was called, and
there is no new SWE-bench or natural historical-bug cohort. The actual SWE-bench
harness uses isolated repository environments~\cite{swebenchharness}; scripted
source mutations are not equivalent. Source-level token counts, when measured
later, will still not establish provider billing or better agent reasoning.

Trust and scope: Future Code remains a single-host supervisor. The guard and TLS
utility are not a distributed consensus protocol or fault-tolerant deployment. A
passing declared suite cannot establish absence of arbitrary defects.
Authorization requires an uncompromised checker, correctly scoped inputs and
protected reference state. A separate integration service must bind the verified
candidate and integration head atomically; no PDF table or root JSON card
substitutes for that step.

\paragraph{Changing the tree is not free.}
Our metric is steady-state refresh traffic. Constructing a new layout requires
planning and initializing its internal records; switching may also create
scheduling or audit overhead. If a proposed tree is expected to save $d>0$ bytes
per future episode and its migration costs $B_{\mathrm{switch}}$ bytes, the
simplest break-even count is $B_{\mathrm{switch}}/d$. This arithmetic is not a
validated online switching policy. A deployable policy should estimate savings on
independent recent batches, account for drift and planning cost in compatible
units, and require a margin before replacement.

\paragraph{A small verification context is not a small total intelligence requirement.}
A conjunction of trusted gate verdicts has an exact compact aggregate. Arbitrary
design tradeoffs, theorem discovery, research assessment, and natural-language
correctness generally do not. An encoder supporting exact recovery of every bit of
an unrestricted $n$-bit state without retrieval must distinguish $2^n$ states and
needs at least $n$ bits. Durable evidence and selective reads relocate information;
they do not make it disappear. HACT therefore makes no claim that every difficult
project can be solved by short independent conversations.

\subsection{Toward a productivity claim: design and evaluation protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
The review (\texttt{HACT\_v5\_Review\_and\_Productivity\_Redesign.pdf})
recommends treating HACT as the evidence substrate of a substantially different
program about \emph{verified-frontier context retirement}, dependency-aware
parallelism, and task granularity. We record that program here as a protocol, not
as a completed experiment, and we give the concrete acceptance gates it must pass.

The central hypothesis is that validated task boundaries make selective forgetting
and reuse safer and more economical than unconditional replay or length-only
truncation. A task-local capsule carries typed fields: task and requirement IDs;
candidate/head IDs and read-set bindings; write scope and interface versions;
verified predicates and evidence references; unresolved questions and
counterexamples; pending messages and ownership; and artifact references, budget,
and next gate. Executable facts, source observations, and unverified model
hypotheses must have different types: a test \PASS{} does not certify the semantic
completeness of a natural-language summary.

A defensible accounting identity (exact under its stated assumptions, not a
guarantee) is that, with $N$ model calls, fixed header $h$, and $g$ retained
tokens per call, complete replay submits $I_{\mathrm{full}}=Nh+gN(N-1)/2$, while
renewing context every $L$ calls with capsule size $s$, extra capsule construction
tokens $F$, and renewal rework $Q$ submits
\begin{equation}I_{\mathrm{renew}}=N(h+s)+gN(L-1)/2+F+Q.\end{equation}
The policy loses when $F$ or $Q$ is large. Against a good observation-masking
baseline~\cite{complexitytrap}, the quadratic history term may already be absent,
so this bookkeeping alone does not establish a productivity win.

Required causal ablations separate context policy from coordination policy and
compare against the falsifying baselines: (i) a strong single agent with
observation masking and focused retrieval; (ii) the same single agent with
periodic summary/reset; (iii) the existing hierarchical Future Code bounded
contexts; (iv) a decentralized verified-board baseline; (v) the proposed
retirement with a fixed scheduler; and (vi) the scheduler without frontier-aware
retirement. All main arms must use equal total budgets and the same final checks,
sandbox and protected state; a many-agent system must not receive a multiplied
budget unnoticed. The primary quality variable is whole-project acceptance under
a frozen independent contract, reported as \emph{cost per accepted project} next
to acceptance rate, with deadline success and time-to-acceptance distributions
retaining failures as censored observations.

A practical go/no-go gate, proposed (not measured) is at least a 20\% reduction in
cost at a prespecified quality margin, with the confidence interval, sample-size
plan, timeout handling and failed-run accounting fixed before outcomes. No benefit
over masking, verified-board and fixed-scheduler baselines means the expensive new
controller should not be promoted. If HACT's tree adds no improvement after
flattening evidence while keeping the contract, HACT remains optional and the
productivity gain, if any, belongs to the context/scheduling layer. Until this live
cohort is run, this direction is labeled a design and evaluation protocol; there is
no justified replacement numerical abstract.
