\begin{abstract}
When can an agent stop remembering its execution history without losing
the current evidence and obligations it needs to finish correctly? We
answer this question at the mechanism level with a pre-registered,
frozen-protocol study: six hundred thirty deterministic runs over ten
context policies---full replay, observation masking, periodic
summary/reset, bounded hierarchical pages, a verified status board,
verified-frontier retirement with typed capsules, and factorial
scheduler ablations---across three registry sizes, three dependency
families, and seven seeds, with temporal persistence and obligation
lifetimes. Three closure rules were declared before data generation.
The result is deliberately asymmetric. \emph{Null closure triggered}:
frontier retirement does not beat strong observation masking on
submitted bytes (the pre-registered 20\% gate failed), so we claim no
byte-cost productivity win. What retirement buys is correctness and
fewer retrieval operations: periodic summary/reset---the cheapest
policy on submitted bytes---fails the acceptance contract on 42 of 63
runs (30{,}820 stale reads; 280 lost obligations), whereas
verified-frontier retirement, masking, and the verified board accept
63/63; frontier retirement cuts on-demand retrieval operations by
48.3\% versus masking (66.8\% on clustered work), pre-registering the
hypothesis that live-agent savings come from tool-call round trips
rather than bytes. The scheduler ablation is byte-neutral by design and
reduces makespan rounds by 87.4\%, a separate dimension from cost. The
HACT certificate-tree evidence layout survives only conditionally:
against a steel-manned flat ledger that takes the better of full and
delta exports at identical record cost, the learned tree reduces
held-out evidence bytes by 59.8\% pooled, but \emph{loses} on sparse
clustered and independent deltas ($+88.7\%$ and $+49.2\%$) and wins on
dense global updates ($-87.1\%$); we demote it from the paper's center
to a conditional component with an explicit decision rule. All
experiments are synthetic and mechanism-level: no hosted model was
called, no token or billing claim is made, and the live-agent cohort
remains future work with its protocol pre-registered here.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} bounded evidence; verification contracts; bandwidth-limited logging; completion hyperedges; cost-aware layout selection.
