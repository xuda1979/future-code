\begin{abstract}
When can an agent stop remembering its execution history without losing
the current evidence and obligations it needs to finish correctly---and
in which cost regime does the answer pay? We answer at the mechanism
level with a pre-registered, factorial, frozen-protocol study of
context retirement: 1{,}176 runs crossing seven retention policies with
a scheduler factor, plus a 160-episode long-horizon sub-cohort, across
four registry sizes, three dependency families, and seven seeds, with
temporal persistence, obligation lifetimes, three-phase accounting, a
five-episode capsule audit against stream ground truth, and two frozen
cost regimes (bytes; bytes plus a per-round-trip constant). The result
that survives is a \emph{conversion rule with a regime-conditional
payoff}. An agent may retire its history only after converting it into
verified interface bindings, a typed pending-obligation set, and
declared dependencies; lossy conversion measurably fails (periodic
summary/reset accepts 28 of 84 runs at the new scale, with 109{,}761
stale reads and 385 lost obligations), while verified-frontier
retirement accepts 84/84 and passes the capsule audit with zero
defective service. The payoff is regime-conditional and was
pre-registered as such: frontier retirement is cheaper than masking on
\emph{both} axes---bytes by \RetwoBytesDelta{} pooled (76 of 84
cells, sign test $p{=}5{\times}10^{-15}$; below the frozen 20\% byte
gate, so no byte-threshold win is claimed) and round-trip-regime cost
by \RetwoCostOReduction{} (84 of 84 cells, $p{=}10^{-25}$), cutting
retrieval operations \RetwoOpsReduction{} pooled and
\RetwoLongOpsReduction{} at four times the horizon; a closed-form
break-even rule (Proposition~\ref{prop:breakeven}) replaces the
sensitivity sweep and gives a deployment pilot an exact go/no-go. The
conversion rule is also \emph{sufficient} within the model
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sufficiency}): under the engine's stream
invariants and reliable retrieval, the frontier policy provably
incurs zero staleness and zero obligation loss. The scheduler
factor is byte-neutral by design and measured at \RetwoSEffectOnBytes{}
leak with an \RetwoMakespanReduction{} makespan reduction. Grouped
certificate layouts survive only as a conditional component selected by
measured update density (dense stratum pass; \RetwoDensityRegret{}
decision-rule regret), and a demand-driven adaptive-coordination
variant was evaluated against pre-registered promotion gates and
\emph{rejected} ($\RetwoAdaptiveReduction{}$ versus frontier; the
correctness-preserving mechanism does not need it). Pre-specified
exploratory analyses (labeled as such) stress the result: a
bounded-recency comparator is cheaper than frontier below a stated
break-even price but leaves 69.0\% of due obligations outside its
retention, quantifying what it delegates to the retrieval layer; and a
replay of a real repository's change history through the same engine
preserves the operations advantage (23.0\% cut) with the density rule
matching the oracle on its first real trace. All experiments are
mechanism-level: no hosted model was called, no token or billing claim
is made, and the live-agent cohort remains future work with its
protocol pre-registered here.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} bounded evidence; verification contracts; bandwidth-limited logging; completion hyperedges; cost-aware layout selection.
