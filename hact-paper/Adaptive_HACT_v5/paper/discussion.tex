\section{Implications and Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Operational implication.}
HACT should be selected only after the evidence service is selected. For a trusted final verdict, the root projection is the natural representation; for final per-check states, a flat ledger may suffice. When a system already requires bounded hierarchical diagnostics, learned-order balanced8 is a low-compilation-cost candidate and the incumbent remains a first-class fallback. The journal replication makes the condition more precise: stable clustered completion structure produces a large and repeatable benefit, whereas independent and global controls do not. A deployment should therefore cache layouts, measure drift, and retune only when new evidence justifies the migration price.

\paragraph{Validation reduces risk; it does not create a no-regret selector.}
The independent controls are important because they falsify a stronger, tempting claim. Even with a separate validation cohort, sampling variation can select a learned layout that is slightly worse on held-out independent waves. The observed mean effects are near zero and their bootstrap intervals cross zero, but 6/20, 3/20, and 6/20 seeds regress at the three sizes. Validation avoids 4, 6, and 4 regressions relative to unconditional deployment of the learned proposal; it does not eliminate them. Production use therefore needs an economic switching threshold and, where regression tolerance is strict, larger validation evidence or an explicit statistical/non-inferiority guard.

\paragraph{Distribution shift is a first-class systems concern.}
When the latent cluster permutation changes, the previously useful layout loses essentially all benefit and is slightly harmful on average at 1,024 checks. Retuning restores a positive result in all sixty shifted replications. This supports the architectural separation between a stable semantic registry and a mutable layout plane: adaptation is valuable precisely because layout state can become stale while the verification contract remains unchanged. It also argues against continuously self-modifying the acceptance semantics in response to the same telemetry.

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
The primary objective is serialized hierarchical-evidence cost under an explicit packet model. Padded bytes, canonical bytes, compressed bytes, diagnostic pages, model tokens, wall-clock latency, and end-to-end agent productivity are different quantities. We measure the first four where available and do not convert bytes to tokens by a heuristic ratio. The break-even model uses declared goodput and incremental work; sensitivity scenarios are planning inputs rather than measured WAN latency. Whole-epoch compression changes streaming semantics, so a smaller archive is not automatically a better online service.

\paragraph{Internal and statistical validity.}
The journal extension was precommitted before execution, uses disjoint train/validation/held-out draws, and treats each seed-level workload as one statistical unit. Twenty seeds per cell are a substantial improvement over the original three, but the bootstrap intervals still describe this frozen generator rather than an unrestricted software population. Directional sign tests support the predeclared clustered/retuning hypotheses; they are not used as a post-hoc discovery screen over every reported metric. The failed first independent audit is retained in provenance: it exposed an error in the verifier's initialization accounting, no failed-run result was committed, and the correction changed the verifier only. The final independent recomputation and tamper test pass.

\paragraph{External validity.}
The synthetic generator deliberately isolates completion locality, null regimes, scale, and a controlled permutation shift. The separate source-derived evidence spans NetworkX, Toolz, and Future Code: 33 held-out confirmation cases show 28 positive exact-layout results, and twelve practical-layout codec replays show compressed-byte/page reductions. These cohorts broaden the evidence beyond synthetic waves, but they still cover only three software systems and reuse archived verification records. They do not measure a new natural-bug population, live model-generated repairs, provider billing, or physical-WAN behavior. A stronger future external-validity study should freeze a multi-repository natural-history benchmark and run the same semantic contract, codec, and layout policies on newly collected candidate streams.

\paragraph{Security and semantic boundary.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} is conditional on a complete approved predicate set, correctly bound dependencies, a trusted checker, collision-resistant identity binding, and atomic local epoch/generation checks. HACT does not provide remote attestation, Byzantine consensus, non-repudiation, or an OS sandbox. A separate integration service must atomically bind the verified candidate to the integration head before merge or deployment. Evidence-layout independence protects the meaning of the declared contract; it cannot make an incomplete contract complete.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and artifact scope.}
The repository retains the frozen journal contract, producer, independent auditor, exact seed sets, environment metadata, per-workload orders and totals, hashes of deterministic generated waves, generated tables, and journal figure. Historical v3--v5 outputs remain versioned rather than rewritten into the new cohort. This separation allows reviewers to distinguish reproduction of old claims, new journal-extension evidence, and historical controls.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Adaptive HACT turns hierarchical verification evidence into a workload-adaptive representation problem under a semantic invariant. Stable identities and generation fencing separate authorization from layout; completion hyperedges provide the signal needed to exploit higher-order locality without allowing the learner to write trusted outcomes.

The precommitted journal extension strengthens the empirical claim substantially. Across sixty clustered replications, validated HACT improves every seed, with mean held-out padded-cost reductions of 23.56\%, 10.66\%, and 5.26\% at 128, 512, and 1,024 checks and narrow seed-level bootstrap intervals. Marginal-frequency and random-order baselines are materially weaker. Null controls show no systematic benefit and reveal that validation is risk-reducing rather than no-regret. Under a controlled distribution shift, stale layouts lose their advantage while retuning restores 23.58\%, 10.99\%, and 5.41\% mean reductions with no observed regressions. Previously frozen source-derived confirmation and codec studies show that layout-sensitive cost is not confined to the synthetic generator, while also exposing substantial case-to-case variation.

These results support a specific systems claim: when hierarchical verification evidence is required and completion structure is stable enough to learn, HACT can reduce its representation and delivery cost while preserving the declared acceptance semantics. They do not establish universal gains in agent intelligence, token use, WAN latency, or software quality. The separation between semantic safety, workload-sensitive representation, drift-aware adaptation, and deployment economics is the paper's central contribution.
