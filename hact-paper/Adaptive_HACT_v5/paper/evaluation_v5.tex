The frozen v5 contract fixes three registry sizes (128, 512, 1,024), three completion families (clustered, independent, global), and three seeds (55011--55013). Each of the 27 workloads contains 128 training epochs, 128 validation epochs, and 256 held-out epochs. Random draws are sequential disjoint samples; deterministic global observations naturally coincide. The catalogue is fit only on training data, selected on validation, and reported on held-out data. Cold strict-epoch construction is included in every objective. The nine size/family summaries report seed means and observed ranges; with three seeds per cell we do not turn episode counts into pseudo-replicated confidence claims.

The source study re-aggregates twelve archived candidates, four each from NetworkX, Toolz, and Future Code, under three layouts. All three representations consume the same trusted check records, execution sequence, and batches of eight. The 36 outputs are layout replays, not fresh checker executions or new autonomous repairs. Historical v3/v4 records remain in the artifact under their original versioned provenance; they are not merged into the v5 empirical counts.

Clustered completions yield mean held-out reductions of 23.43\%, 10.75\%, and 5.56\% at 128, 512, and 1,024 checks. More importantly than the means, every clustered seed is positive: the observed ranges are 22.40--24.45\%, 10.06--11.13\%, and 5.16--5.80\%. Global updates yield exactly zero improvement in all three sizes. Independent updates yield zero except for one 512-check seed, producing a 0.07\% mean and a 0.00--0.20\% range. The experiment therefore supports a conditional claim: ordering is useful when completion waves contain stable locality, and the validation stage can retain the incumbent when they do not.

The practical path invokes no tree DP. At 1,024 checks, catalogue fitting still takes about three seconds on the recorded host, so learned layouts belong off the agent's per-action path and should be cached. The decreasing percentage with registry size is consistent with substantial layout-independent cold construction in this fixture; it is not presented as a scaling law.

The first synthetic execution exceeded its tool time limit after persisting 26 complete records. No worker remained; explicit resume skipped those immutable records and completed the final frozen case without changing inputs, selection, or seeds. The interruption record remains part of the artifact provenance.

Learned balanced layouts reduce whole-epoch compressed bytes by 41.84\% for NetworkX~\cite{networkx}, 4.36\% for Toolz~\cite{toolz}, and 18.53\% for Future Code. Across all twelve candidates, batch-compressed application bytes fall from 416,046 for fixed8 to 299,081 for learned balanced. Learned exact uses 278,831 bytes, leaving 11.68\%, 1.52\%, and 6.67\% project-level headroom relative to balanced. The practical compiler is therefore a Pareto choice: it removes cubic tree synthesis from the default path while deliberately giving up some byte optimality available to the fixed-order exact reference.

Semantic counts, verdicts, and local authorization agree across all 36 replays. Root-status text is byte-identical in every layout triple and occupies 593--594 UTF-8 bytes, directly validating the negative control in Equation~\eqref{eq:rootcontext}. Exhaustive diagnostic pages total 148 for fixed8, 95 for learned balanced, and 87 for learned exact, so the practical layout uses 35.81\% fewer pages than fixed8. The largest page is 16,364 bytes under a 16,384-byte cap. These page counts describe an exhaustive diagnostic service; they do not imply that a model will request every page.

\subsection{RQ4: break-even depends on the deployed service}
The fixed-to-balanced whole-epoch compressed difference is 116,965 bytes per twelve-candidate cohort. Under the explicitly assumed goodput of 64 KiB/s and $E=0$, Equation~\eqref{eq:latency} converts that difference to 1.784744 seconds of gross delivery time per cohort copy. This is service-model arithmetic from measured bytes, not a measured WAN speedup.

The table exposes the operating region rather than selecting a universal winner. At 64 KiB/s, for example, an assumed 60 seconds of extra cohort work requires 34 repetitions of the same delivery service before modeled savings become strictly positive. New candidates incur new preparation and cannot reuse that amortization. The artifact stores 27 scenarios spanning three codecs, three rates, and three extra-cost assumptions; negative outcomes are retained, and mismatched or incomplete services return \texttt{INCOMPARABLE} or \texttt{UNKNOWN} rather than an invented benefit.

\subsection{Controls, software validation, and independent reconstruction}
Historical controls remain useful only in their original roles. Earlier receiver-paced loopback experiments showed that compression itself can dominate layout choice, and earlier harness timings showed no end-to-end speedup from the strict wrapper. Because those measurements use different cohorts and services, this manuscript does not subtract them from the new replay data. The block compiler's historical 1.54--5.34\% held-out penalty to same-order balanced8 is retained as the ablation that motivated the simpler default.

The v5 prototype passes 329 tests, including 76 new cases, while the unchanged Future Code suite passes 263 tests separately. New tests cover no-DP practical compilation, service mismatch, missing costs, exact break-even, whole-text token admission, TLS trust failures, stable-ID migration, and deliberate data tampering. A separate auditor imports no producer optimizer, selector, or codec and independently reconstructs initialization/wave costs for all 27 workloads, all 36 serialized replays, codec sizes, diagnostic pages, 27 planning scenarios, and 131 summary numerical comparisons. This is independent arithmetic and provenance reconstruction, not third-party certification.
