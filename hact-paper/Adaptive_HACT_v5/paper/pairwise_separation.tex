\begin{theorem}[Pairwise summaries do not identify an optimal tree]\label{thm:pairwise-optimum}
For $H=512$, $S=320$ and maximum arity eight, there are two completion-set
distributions on nine checks with identical singleton and pairwise
probabilities but disjoint globally optimal tree sets. This holds over all
valid leaf permutations and tree shapes for the persistent refresh cost,
with any nonnegative multiple of full construction cost added.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the parity distributions $P,Q$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:pairwise} on
$\{0,1,2\}$ and include six additional checks in every completion set.
All nine singleton probabilities and all pairwise joint probabilities agree.
Every subtree containing an additional check is hit with probability one.
An internal subtree containing only parity checks is hit with probability
at least $3/4$.

Let $I$ be the number of internal nodes. Since the sum of internal arities
is $9+I-1$, its full construction cost is
\[
 W(T)=512I+320(9+I-1)=832I+2560.
\]
The arity cap requires $I\ge2$. At most two internal nodes can contain only
the three parity checks; their combined packet cost is at most $2304$
(two binary nodes). Consequently expected refresh cost is at least
$W(T)-2304/4$. For $I\ge3$ this is at least $4480$ bytes, and construction
cost is at least $5056$ bytes.

With $I=2$, the tree consists of the root and a single internal child, and
$W(T)=4224$. A child containing an additional check gives expected refresh
cost $4224$. A two-check parity child gives $4224-1152/4=3936$ under either
distribution. A three-check parity child gives $4224-1472/4=3856$ under $P$
and $4224$ under $Q$. Thus $P$ uniquely groups the triple, whereas $Q$ groups
one of its three pairs. No other shape improves these values. Their
construction costs agree and are smaller than those of all $I\ge3$ trees,
so adding any nonnegative construction multiplier preserves the separation.
\end{proof}

A rule that sees only these pairwise probabilities receives identical
input under $P$ and $Q$ and therefore cannot always identify an optimum.
This is an identifiability result, not a claim about a particular clustering
implementation or about typical software workloads. It uses persistent
refresh events; it does not assert that a partially observed event is a
complete strict verification epoch.

The independent reference in \texttt{experiments\_v10/exact\_separation.py}
uses integer probability numerators and a subset recurrence over all root
set partitions. It solves 511 nonempty subsets and considers 115,462 feasible
root partitions per distribution and construction case. It finds one
unordered optimum under $P$ and three under $Q$. These counts treat sibling
orders as equivalent. Separate literal tree enumeration checks the reference
on smaller instances. This analytical witness is distinct from the frozen
nine-check experiment, which enumerates permutations of one balanced shape.
