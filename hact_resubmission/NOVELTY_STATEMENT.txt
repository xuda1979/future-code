The manuscript reports completely new research results, not previously reported. It proves an information limit for adaptive verification evidence: exact singleton and pairwise probabilities can leave globally optimal certificate trees unidentified for every fanout of at least three and every affine positive-slot packet price, with sharp minimax regret. A complete-epoch construction extends this to fixed-eight batching within a two-layout class. It introduces Adaptive HACT, separating a protected semantic registry from generation-fenced certificate trees, and a local publication invariant that preserves acceptance through migration, with a conditional adoption rule covering calibration, shift, and installation costs. These are new theorems, a new mechanism, and new negative results, not previously reported.

Criterion (b): The manuscript does not extend prior work solely with additional proofs or algorithms or other such details presented for completeness, or additional empirical results, or minor enhancements or variants of the results presented in the prior work. Its central information limit, the registry/tree separation, and the safety-preserving migration invariant are substantive new contributions; the empirical replay study supports rather than constitutes the novelty.

The paper cites established work on tree optimization, hypergraphs, and verification frameworks, and the authors do not claim those previously published ingredients as their new contributions here.
